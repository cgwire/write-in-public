# Contributing to The Studio Life

Thanks for helping. You don't need to write anything polished; the editor works your material into the articles and credits you for it.

## How to submit

Submissions are accepted in two places only:

- **GitHub:** for a correction, click *Suggest an edit* on the article. This opens a pull request for you.
- **[Discord](https://discord.gg/4aPk855Yd):** post in `#blog-submissions`. For anonymous testimony, DM **@bazamel** instead.

Anything sent by email, newsletter reply or social DM won't be picked up.

## What to send

From smallest to largest: a **correction**, **testimony** (named or anonymous), **data** (rates, budgets, headcounts, timelines), or an **interview** if it's easier to talk than type.

## Sourcing

- **Give context with every number:** territory, year, discipline (2D, 3D, VFX, motion, games) and studio size. A day rate without these can't be used.
- **Say how you know it.** Your own books, a bid you saw, an industry survey, or a best guess. All are welcome, but they're labelled differently on the page.
- **Don't share what you're not allowed to.** If a figure is under NDA, round it, range it or leave it out. Strip client names unless they're already public.
- **Disagreement is fine.** If your experience contradicts an article, don't hesitate to say so. We like nuance.

## Credit

- Everyone who contributes is named in the article's frontmatter and on the page, down to a single fixed figure.
- Revisers are named in the changelog of the version they worked on.
- You choose how you're credited (full name, first name, studio, role, or anonymous) **after** you've seen how your material is used, before it publishes.

## Anonymity

- Anonymous contributions go through a DM to @bazamel, never a public channel or pull request.
- Your identity stays with the editor. It is not written into the repo, the frontmatter or the commit history.
- Details that could identify you (city, studio size, a named project) are generalised with your agreement.
- You can withdraw anonymous testimony at any time before publication.

## Review and versioning

- Every draft opens as a pull request and stays open for comment before publishing. Reviewing drafts from your territory or discipline is one of the most useful things you can do.
- Articles ship at 1.0 and stay open. Changes land as new versions with a visible changelog.
- Articles on tooling, AI, real-time and funding programmes are re-checked regularly. If you spot something outdated, flag it.
